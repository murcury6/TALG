# Trading tab: explicit execution and desk-built rules

The current **Model → Paper session** view uses the [rapid statistical basket and model bricks](RAPID_PAPER.md). The opening-range section below documents the older saved-session format and prior experiment.

The **TRADE** tab shows current positions, open orders and manual trading in one compact workspace. Positions refresh every 15 seconds while the tab is visible. **New order** opens a labeled ticket; **Submit preview** retains the separate confirmation. **Model**, immediately after Trade, contains trading code, model-defined controls and ticker outputs. **News** owns news code, its workbook, symbol ratings and model-defined update events. See [Model language and workspaces](MODEL_LANGUAGE.md). It uses the connected Alpaca account mode: `Paper account` sends requests only to `paper-api.alpaca.markets`; `Live account` sends requests only to `api.alpaca.markets`. [Alpaca requires distinct credentials and domains for paper and live trading](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/v1.1/docs/authentication-1). Confirm the mode in the tab before every submission. TALG never changes a live key into a paper key. Switching modes in Settings requires entering that mode's own key and secret; unsaved mode selections do not reroute the current connection. Only one credential pair is stored at a time.

The ticket supports whole-share U.S. stock buys and sells, with a configurable maximum notional capped at $2,500 per order. It uses a DAY limit order, not an unbounded market order. A preview checks Alpaca's market clock, account trading status, buying power or existing long position, and a timestamped quote. It refuses stale quotes, a closed market, delayed SIP data, short-selling through this ticket, and a limit-price notional above the cap. These checks are not a guarantee of execution or price; the broker can reject the order, and an accepted order can remain open or fill later. [Alpaca's order endpoint](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/v1.1/reference/postorder) documents limit prices, day time-in-force, and order states. TALG does not retry an uncertain submission automatically. Use **Refresh** to inspect open orders, **Check last order** to see whether the most recently submitted order has filled or changed state, and **Cancel order** to request cancellation; a cancel request is not proof that an order did not fill. [Alpaca's cancellation behavior](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/reference/deleteorderbyorderid-1).

Live orders require typing `LIVE BUY SYMBOL QTY` or `LIVE SELL SYMBOL QTY` exactly in a confirmation dialog. Manual paper orders require a separate yes/no confirmation. A preview expires after one minute; submission repeats the preflight checks and rejects a changed limit price. Saving a script, opening a desk, receiving a quote, or evaluating a true rule never submits an order. General strategy scripts still run only through explicit evaluation. The separate **Paper strategy** described below supports one bounded, explicitly armed, paper-only pilot.

## Paper strategy: opening-range v1

Inside TRADE, choose **Paper strategy**, select the session date, and press **Check & arm paper strategy**. Its confirmation authorizes that bounded session without per-order prompts. Arming performs read-only account, asset, market-calendar, position and open-order checks. It does not place an order before the scheduled start.

The pilot seeks profitable long trades in SPY, with at most one share held and $1,000 limit-price notional per order. It starts observing at 09:35 Eastern. Between 09:45 and 11:00, a completed minute must freshly cross above the 09:30–09:45 range high plus two basis points, close above its open and session VWAP, and satisfy the risk filters. All 15 opening minute bars must exist; missing observations are not invented. The connected free IEX feed is used without upgrading to paid data. No signal means no trade. At most two round trips are allowed, with five minutes between an exit fill and another entry.

Initial per-share risk is the greater of the breakout close minus the opening low or 0.2% of the close; skip when above 0.8%. The stop trigger is the actual entry fill minus this risk, and the target is the fill plus twice risk. After a one-risk-unit advance, trail one risk unit below the highest observed bid. Exit after two completed closes below session VWAP once held ten minutes, after sixty minutes, or at noon. Quote-based exits are checked every fifteen seconds and do not wait for the entry cooldown. Skip entries with spreads above five basis points or excessive price movement. New entries stop after two consecutive losses or estimated daily net P&L of -$8; planned risk plus assumed costs must fit the remaining $8 budget. These are triggers, not guaranteed maximum losses.

Orders are regular-hours DAY limits at ask plus $0.01 for buys and bid minus $0.01 for sells. Exits are managed by the running app; no broker-hosted protective stop is installed. Gaps, latency, an unavailable quote, a closed app, or an unfilled limit can cause losses beyond the trigger. No live endpoint exists in this runner. The older deterministic smoke-test plan remains readable for saved-session compatibility; the UI arms `opening-range-v1`.

Research files under `work/paper-test/research/` preserve minute observations, trade-by-trade replays, and both candidates' summaries. In 41 historical IEX sessions, the first momentum candidate lost about $9.96 after assumed costs; the opening-range candidate made about $5.51 across 22 one-share trades, but lost about $1.93 in the recent six-trade segment. This second candidate was explored after the first result, so the recent segment is not an untouched validation set. Assumed costs are two basis points per side plus $0.01 per order. This is exploratory evidence, not a proven edge: bar replay cannot reproduce quote spreads, app polling, intrabar trailing, actual limit fills, or all live risk filters. P&L in the app separately shows actual fill-price gross P&L and net with these assumed costs.

Keep the armed TALG window open and the computer awake. The test runs inside the app, not through a Windows task or external bot. It cannot trade while TALG is closed. A saved active session can resume after a restart; a process lock prevents simultaneous TALG windows from running it twice. Restarting an expired flat session finishes it rather than replaying missed orders. Switching away from the paper connection pauses it.

**Stop & exit position** prevents new entries, requests cancellation of an outstanding entry, waits for the broker's terminal order status, and attempts to sell only this session's recorded filled quantity during regular market hours. Cancellation does not mean an order did not fill. A timeout requests cancellation after two minutes; a canceled partial entry is exited for its actual filled quantity. A failed/partially filled exit, changed position, conflicting SPY order, account mismatch, invalid/stale exit quote, or uncertain submission pauses the session for review. Unavailable entry data skips entry; unavailable trend data does not disable quote-based exits. A paused session may retain a position or open order; inspect Alpaca before further action. No automatic resubmission is made after uncertain outcomes. Do not trade SPY manually in the same account while this session is running.

`work/paper-test/session.json` stores the versioned plan, phase, paper account identity, owned quantity, risk levels, P&L and outstanding order identifiers. `work/paper-test/events-YYYY-MM-DD.jsonl` records signals, arming, submit intents, acknowledgments, terminal fills/cancellations, exit triggers, P&L, completion and errors. Keys are never written to these files. The submission intent is saved before the POST so an interrupted submission cannot silently become a duplicate after restart. An unresolved paused session cannot be overwritten through the arm button; reconcile it first.

## Strategy script

Scripts are stored on the USB under `work/strategies/*.strategy`. **Save Script** only saves text. A usable block workflow is: build and test a named R indicator in **INDICATORS**, choose **USE IN TRADING** to add it as an input, use **+ EQUATION** to make a named calculation (including percent difference between two signals), then use **+ CONDITION** to combine observed fields, inputs, and equations into Buy/Sell logic. Each action edits the visible strategy script, so the result is inspectable and can still be edited directly. The condition builder joins a new condition to an existing side with AND or OR; it does not silently replace that rule. **Evaluate Rule** fetches observed Alpaca bars, computes the named R indicators, optionally reads recent validated Models-panel output, evaluates the rules, and prepares a trade preview only if exactly one side is true. It evaluates the latest observation, **not** a historical backtest or an unattended trading loop. The rule engine supports numeric `+ - * /`, parentheses, comparison operators `> >= < <= == !=`, and Boolean `and`, `or`, `not`. It does not execute arbitrary Java or R expressions. Indicator files are trusted local R code and run in a process without Alpaca credential environment variables; they are not a general sandbox.

```text
name Trend Volume
ticker AAPL
range 6M
bars 1Day
input trend Indicator(ema_34)
input pressure Indicator(volume_ratio_20)
let edge = close / trend - 1
buy edge > 0.01 and pressure > 1.10
sell edge < -0.01
qty 1
```

Built-in variables are the latest observed `open`, `high`, `low`, `close`, `volume`, `vwap`, and `trades` when Alpaca provides them. `Indicator(name)` reads `work/indicators/name.R`, using the same `talg_indicator(bars)` contract as desk charts; older `Custom(name)` scripts remain valid. The supplied research desk includes `ema_34`, `volume_ratio_20`, and `price_zscore_20`. `Model(name,metric)` reads the latest result produced by **RUN ON ALPACA BARS** in a Models panel, for example `input forecast Model(forecast_v1,expected_return)`. Its source checksum, stock, feed, and timestamps must match and be recent; if the model has not been run or is stale, evaluation fails instead of making up a number. Strategy inputs and rule values are displayed with their observation time before a trade can be confirmed.

The supplied `Trend Volume` rules are *examples of wiring*, not validated investment signals. In particular, a daily bar may be incomplete during the trading day, IEX is not a consolidated feed, and a signal based on it can change before close. Test your own rules with point-in-time data, transaction costs, slippage, corporate actions, and out-of-sample evaluation before any live use. TALG provides no return guarantee, portfolio-level risk engine, or automatic protective exit.
